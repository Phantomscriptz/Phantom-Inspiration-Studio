# Phantom: Monetization & Content Strategy

Last reviewed: 2026-07-31. Platform eligibility, availability, countries, fees, and payout methods change often; verify the linked official program terms before enrolling.

## Product principle

Phantom should help a creator make **original, useful, reviewable** content. It must not encourage copied videos, fake engagement, mass-produced near-duplicates, undisclosed affiliate links, or incentives for viewers to click/watch ads. Those tactics risk demonetization, account loss, affiliate termination, and loss of audience trust.

There is no guaranteed-view or guaranteed-income feature. The measurable goal is to improve the creator's process: stronger hooks, original scripts, relevant visuals, responsible disclosures, and feedback from real analytics.

## What successful faceless channels do well

1. Pick a narrow viewer promise, not a broad "faceless" category. Example: original 30-second resilience lessons for people building habits.
2. Start with a precise first-second hook, then deliver the promised payoff. Avoid unsupported statistics, generic quote cards, and bait-and-switch titles.
3. Match the visuals to the narration: each sentence should introduce a meaningful visual change, motion, or piece of proof.
4. Use original commentary/research and licensed assets. AI tools may assist, but a human reviews accuracy, rights, tone, and uniqueness.
5. Make platform-specific packaging: title, description, thumbnail, captions, disclosure, and sensible relevant tags.
6. Run small experiments and learn from retention, saves, comments, click-through rate, and subscribers—not from generic "viral" claims.

## Direct audience income

These are generally healthier than trying to maximize ads from a new channel:

- Patreon, Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee: memberships, tips, bonus material.
- Gumroad, Payhip, Fourthwall, Shopify: templates, guides, digital downloads, merchandise.
- Substack: a written newsletter that expands the video topic.
- Sponsorships: only after an audience and a clearly defined niche exist.

Use a real value exchange. For an inspiration channel, examples might be a habit-tracker template, a short weekly newsletter, or a source list—never a vague paywall with no useful product.

## Affiliate networks and partners

Use only a partner that is genuinely relevant to the exact video. A book/journal/tool link belongs under a related video; an unrelated AI-tool link does not belong under every upload.

### Current in-app catalog corrections

- ElevenLabs: keep only if the channel content actually uses/recommends it and the current affiliate terms are verified.
- Runway/Midjourney: do not promise commissions until the program is confirmed directly with the company.
- PropellerAds: not an appropriate default video-description affiliate. It monetizes an owned website, can be intrusive, and should never be auto-appended to video descriptions.

Every affiliate description needs a plain disclosure, for example: "Some links below may be affiliate links. I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you."

The US FTC's disclosure guidance is a useful baseline even when the creator lives elsewhere: [Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers).

## Payout expectations

Payout method is set by the program, not Phantom. Common methods are bank transfer, PayPal, Stripe/Stripe Connect, Payoneer, or checks. Cash App and Venmo are not dependable business payout rails. Crypto payouts exist on some smaller services but are volatile, jurisdiction-dependent, and should never be the core business model.

Never pay for, promise, or incentivize artificial views, ad clicks, follows, or comments. Do not use paid-to-watch, crypto faucet, click-exchange, or "traffic" websites. They undermine platform and advertising rules.

## Asset rules

- Do not download/reuse another creator's video simply because it is publicly viewable.
- Prefer original footage, original AI-generated visuals, or assets with a license that permits the planned commercial use.
- Pexels, Pixabay, Mixkit, and Unsplash can be useful sources, but check each asset's license/restrictions and record its source.
- Add an asset ledger to each generated project: source URL, license, creator, date acquired, and where used.

## Roadmap: features that actually improve quality

1. **Review package** (in progress): final MP4, thumbnail, title, description, hashtags, affiliate disclosure, and source notes saved together.
2. **Visual source and rights ledger**: pull licensed stock images/video clips or user assets; record provenance.
3. **Cinematic Motion mode**: free local parallax/depth, camera motion, animated captions, subtle particles, and better timing. This improves quality without claiming to be true 3D.
4. **Optional AI Motion Clips**: 3-8 second image-to-video clips per scene. Make this optional because local models require substantial GPU resources and hosted APIs cost money.
5. **Hook and packaging review**: generate several distinct hooks/titles, flag unsupported claims, and require the creator to pick one.
6. **Brand Kit**: channel name, bio, avatar/banner candidates, fonts, colors, thumbnail templates, and a required human approval step.
7. **Verified analytics dashboard**: platform-specific follower/subscriber/view stats, last-sync time, and post-performance learning. Refresh on launch and about hourly after authentication.
8. **Affiliate relevance rules**: select links based on video topic; never append unrelated links automatically.
9. **Trend Research queue**: import creator-selected topics from legitimate trend sources, score niche fit, and require an original angle before a script is generated.
